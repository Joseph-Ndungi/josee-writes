I finished *Ego Is the Enemy* by Ryan Holiday and I have thoughts. Naturally, I think they are very good thoughts, which is the exact problem the book is about.

## The brutal part

The book is a parade of dead and famous men who succeeded by being humble, wrapped in Stoic quotes older than your great great grandmother's grave. It has the structure of a eulogy that never ends. Every chapter says the same thing: shut up, work hard, stay hungry, and one day you will die anyway.

Here is what bothered me.

**Survivorship bias in a nice suit.** Holiday tells us about the humble genius who won. He does not tell us about the thousands of equally humble people who did everything right and are now working quietly in a cubicle, unnoticed, which is apparently the dream.

**Hindsight is doing the heavy lifting.** Every legend in the book is retroactively declared humble because they won. If they had lost, the same traits would be called a lack of ambition. The book is a horoscope: vague enough to fit anyone, confident enough to sell.

**Repetition.** Same point, new corpse. By chapter four I was not enlightened, I was just attending the same funeral in different outfits.

## The takeaways (annoyingly, they work)

**1. Be a student, always.** The moment you think you have arrived is the moment you start decomposing professionally.

**2. Do the work, skip the performance.** Nobody remembers who talked loudest in the meeting. They remember who shipped. Then they forget you too, because everyone does eventually.

**3. Success is more dangerous than failure.** Failure at least keeps you humble. Success convinces you that you are special, which is how people end up giving TED talks about their morning routine.

**4. Detach from the outcome.** You control effort, not applause. The applause is a fickle crowd that will turn on you the second you stop being useful.

**5. Failure is not a verdict.** It is a bruise. Keep moving. You are going to be dead for a much longer time than you will be embarrassed.

## The verdict

Is the book repetitive, a little smug, and built on cherry picked legends? Yes. Did it make me pause before posting something just to be seen? Also yes, and I hate that.

Ego is the enemy, but it is also the little voice that made me write this review as if my opinion mattered. Which it does not. We are all temporary. Eat, build, ship, repeat.

Happy Life.
